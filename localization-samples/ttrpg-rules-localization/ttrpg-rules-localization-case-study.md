# Fifth Edition TTRPG Rules Localization — Case Study

**Language pair:** English → Brazilian Portuguese  
**Source framework:** System Reference Document 5.1  
**Content type:** TTRPG rules, terminology, instructional text, combat, spellcasting, conditions, and reference documentation  
**My role:** Translator, localization specialist, terminology developer, editor, and linguistic QA reviewer  
**Status:** Portfolio case study — Version 1.0

---

## Executive summary

This case study presents an independent Brazilian Portuguese localization of selected fifth-edition tabletop RPG terminology and rules concepts from the System Reference Document 5.1.

The project focuses on the part of game localization where small wording choices directly affect play. A term must not only sound natural in Portuguese; it must also remain mechanically precise, easy to find in reference material, consistent across hundreds of pages, and immediately understandable at the table.

The localization was designed around five priorities:

- preserve the complete mechanical function of every term;
- use Brazilian Portuguese familiar to tabletop RPG players;
- maintain consistent terminology across character sheets, rules, spells, combat, and conditions;
- distinguish player-friendly language from strict rules language;
- document decisions so the terminology can scale across long-form projects.

This study also demonstrates experience with the type of content commonly found in long TTRPG publications: rules explanations, combat procedures, spellcasting systems, character-facing instructions, condition references, and terminology that repeats across multiple chapters.

## Project context

Rules localization requires more than lexical equivalence. A translated term may appear in:

- a character sheet;
- a spell description;
- a combat rule;
- a monster stat block;
- a class feature;
- a condition reference;
- an adventure instruction;
- a virtual tabletop;
- a glossary;
- a tutorial or quick-reference guide.

A weak decision can create ambiguity across all of these contexts. A strong decision creates a stable term that players can recognise and reuse without relearning the system.

The Brazilian Portuguese terminology in this case study was selected by combining:

- mechanical analysis of the English source;
- familiarity with fifth-edition play;
- established Portuguese-language RPG conventions;
- public Portuguese terminology previously used in Wizards materials;
- readability for both new and experienced players;
- long-document consistency.

## Localization objectives

### 1. Preserve mechanical precision

The translation must never change:

- who performs an action;
- when an effect happens;
- what is rolled;
- which modifier is added;
- how success is determined;
- how long an effect lasts;
- whether an action is optional or mandatory.

### 2. Use terminology familiar to Brazilian players

Where a clear and established Portuguese term already exists, retaining it reduces friction and improves searchability.

Examples include:

- **Classe de Armadura**
- **Pontos de Vida**
- **Bônus de Proficiência**
- **Espaço de Magia**
- **Truque**
- **Vantagem**
- **Desvantagem**

### 3. Keep rules language concise

Rules need to be read quickly during play. The target text should avoid:

- unnecessary synonyms;
- long explanatory clauses;
- hidden conditions;
- inconsistent verb forms;
- literal English syntax.

### 4. Separate similar concepts

The terminology must distinguish concepts such as:

- Atributo and Perícia;
- Salvaguarda and Teste de Atributo;
- Ação and Ação Bônus;
- Resistência and Imunidade;
- Nível de Personagem and Círculo da Magia;
- Pontos de Vida and Pontos de Vida Temporários.

### 5. Support long-form consistency

A preferred term must remain stable across:

- chapter headings;
- rules text;
- tables;
- stat blocks;
- cross-references;
- indexes;
- character sheets;
- glossaries.

### 6. Remain usable aloud

TTRPG rules are often read, explained, or paraphrased during a session. The Portuguese text should sound natural when spoken by a Game Master or player.

- Desvantagem

- Magia

## Key localization decisions

### Ability → Atributo

**Alternatives considered:**

- Habilidade
- Característica
- Capacidade

**Decision:**

**Atributo** was selected because it clearly identifies the six foundational character statistics and avoids confusion with learned abilities, class features, and player skill.

**Habilidade** is a possible direct equivalent, but in Portuguese it can refer broadly to something a character knows how to do. **Atributo** is more precise in a character-sheet context.

**Final usage:**

> Faça um Teste de Atributo usando o modificador relevante.

---

### Saving Throw → Salvaguarda

**Alternatives considered:**

- Teste de Resistência
- Jogada de Salvamento
- Teste de Defesa

**Decision:**

**Salvaguarda** is compact, recognisable in Portuguese-language fifth-edition materials, and distinct from damage **Resistência**.

Using **Teste de Resistência** would create terminology overlap with resistance to damage. **Jogada de Salvamento** is understandable but sounds more literal and less established.

**Final usage:**

> A criatura deve realizar uma Salvaguarda de Destreza.

---

### Speed → Deslocamento

**Alternatives considered:**

- Velocidade
- Movimento
- Distância de Movimento

**Decision:**

**Deslocamento** communicates the distance a creature can move during play rather than its physical velocity.

It also works naturally as a compact character-sheet label.

**Final usage:**

> Seu Deslocamento de caminhada é de 9 metros.

---

### Proficiency Bonus → Bônus de Proficiência

**Alternatives considered:**

- Modificador de Proficiência
- Bônus por Proficiência
- Valor de Proficiência

**Decision:**

**Bônus de Proficiência** is concise, transparent, and already familiar to Brazilian fifth-edition players.

The structure also scales naturally to rules such as:

- add your Proficiency Bonus;
- double your Proficiency Bonus;
- you do not add your Proficiency Bonus.

---

### Spell Slot → Espaço de Magia

**Alternatives considered:**

- Espaço de Feitiço
- Carga de Magia
- Reserva de Magia
- Uso de Magia

**Decision:**

**Espaço de Magia** preserves the resource-slot concept and is familiar in Brazilian Portuguese fifth-edition terminology.

The term remains distinct from the spell itself:

- **Magia** is the effect or entry;
- **Espaço de Magia** is the resource spent to cast it.

---

### Spell Level → Círculo da Magia

**Alternatives considered:**

- Nível da Magia
- Grau da Magia
- Patamar da Magia

**Decision:**

**Círculo da Magia** prevents confusion between a character’s level and a spell’s classification.

For example:

> Um mago de 5º nível pode conjurar magias de até 3º círculo.

The distinction is especially useful in tutorials, character creation, and spellcasting explanations.

---

### Cantrip → Truque

**Alternatives considered:**

- Magia Menor
- Magia Básica
- Encanto
- Sortilégio Menor

**Decision:**

**Truque** is short, familiar, and clearly distinguishes a reusable basic spell from spells that consume resources.

**Final usage:**

> Um Truque pode ser conjurado sem gastar um Espaço de Magia.

---

### Restrained → Contido

**Alternatives considered:**

- Restrito
- Imobilizado
- Preso

**Decision:**

**Contido** describes severe limitation without implying that the creature is completely unable to move or act.

**Imobilizado** would overstate the condition. **Preso** is too broad and could overlap with narrative restraint, cages, or Grappled.

## Rules localization examples

The following examples are independently adapted from fifth-edition SRD mechanics to demonstrate localization decisions without reproducing long source passages.

### Example 1 — Ability Check

**Adapted English**

> When an action has an uncertain outcome, the Game Master can ask for an Ability Check.

**Brazilian Portuguese**

> Quando uma ação tiver um resultado incerto, o Mestre de Jogo pode solicitar um Teste de Atributo.

**Localization notes**

- **Solicitar** is more natural in Brazilian rules language than a literal equivalent of *call for*.
- The condition appears before the instruction.
- **Teste de Atributo** remains a defined term.

---

### Example 2 — Difficulty Class

**Adapted English**

> Roll a d20, add the relevant Ability Modifier, and compare the total with the Difficulty Class.

**Brazilian Portuguese**

> Jogue um d20, adicione o Modificador de Atributo relevante e compare o total com a Classe de Dificuldade.

**Localization notes**

- Dice notation remains unchanged.
- The action sequence uses parallel imperative verbs.
- **Relevante** stays next to the modifier it qualifies.

---

### Example 3 — Saving Throw

**Adapted English**

> A Saving Throw represents an attempt to resist a spell, trap, poison, disease, or similar threat.

**Brazilian Portuguese**

> Uma Salvaguarda representa uma tentativa de resistir a uma magia, armadilha, veneno, doença ou ameaça semelhante.

**Localization notes**

- The sentence remains explanatory rather than procedural.
- **Resistir a** preserves the relationship between the character and the harmful effect.
- Lowercase descriptive nouns are retained because they are not titles in this context.

---

### Example 4 — Attack Roll and Armor Class

**Adapted English**

> An attack hits when the Attack Roll equals or exceeds the target’s Armor Class.

**Brazilian Portuguese**

> Um ataque acerta quando a Jogada de Ataque iguala ou supera a Classe de Armadura do alvo.

**Localization notes**

- **Acerta** is direct and avoids a heavier passive structure.
- **Iguala ou supera** precisely preserves the threshold.
- The target relationship remains explicit.

---

### Example 5 — Advantage

**Adapted English**

> With Advantage, roll two d20s and use the higher result.

**Brazilian Portuguese**

> Com Vantagem, jogue dois d20 e use o resultado mais alto.

**Localization notes**

- The condition comes first for fast reference.
- The dice notation is not pluralised.
- **Resultado mais alto** is clearer than a literal reference to the higher die.

---

### Example 6 — Spell Slots

**Adapted English**

> Casting a levelled spell normally expends a Spell Slot of that level or higher.

**Brazilian Portuguese**

> Conjurar uma magia de 1º círculo ou superior normalmente gasta um Espaço de Magia do mesmo círculo ou de um círculo superior.

**Localization notes**

- The Portuguese sentence makes the level relationship explicit.
- **Gasta** is more direct than a formal alternative such as *consome*.
- **Círculo** prevents confusion with character level.

---

### Example 7 — Concentration

**Adapted English**

> Taking damage while concentrating may force the caster to make a Constitution Saving Throw.

**Brazilian Portuguese**

> Sofrer dano enquanto mantém a Concentração pode exigir que o conjurador realize uma Salvaguarda de Constituição.

**Localization notes**

- **Mantém a Concentração** is more natural than a literal participial structure.
- **Pode exigir** preserves conditionality.
- The acting subject remains explicit.

---

### Example 8 — Opportunity Attack

**Adapted English**

> A creature may use its Reaction to make an Opportunity Attack when an enemy leaves its reach.

**Brazilian Portuguese**

> Uma criatura pode usar sua Reação para realizar um Ataque de Oportunidade quando um inimigo sai de seu alcance.

**Localization notes**

- **Realizar um ataque** works naturally in rules prose.
- The triggering condition remains at the end.
- Reação and Ataque de Oportunidade remain distinct defined concepts.

## Units, numbers, and notation

### Metric conversion

For a Brazilian Portuguese edition, imperial distances should be converted into game-friendly metric values.

Preferred conversions include:

| Imperial | Metric localization |
|---|---|
| 5 feet | 1,5 metro |
| 10 feet | 3 metros |
| 15 feet | 4,5 metros |
| 20 feet | 6 metros |
| 30 feet | 9 metros |
| 60 feet | 18 metros |

The objective is consistency and usability on common battle grids, not scientific precision.

### Dice notation

Keep standard notation unchanged:

- d20
- d6
- 2d8
- 1d10 + 3

Do not insert spaces inside a dice expression.

### Abbreviations

Define abbreviations on first use:

- Classe de Armadura (**CA**)
- Classe de Dificuldade (**CD**)
- Pontos de Vida (**PV**)
- peça de ouro (**po**)

### Decimal formatting

Use the Brazilian decimal comma:

- 1,5 metro
- 4,5 metros

## Rules style guide

### Defined terms

Use consistent capitalisation when a term is being treated as a formal game concept in the case study or glossary.

Examples:

- Teste de Atributo
- Salvaguarda
- Classe de Armadura
- Espaço de Magia
- Concentração

A final publisher style guide may choose sentence case in running text, but the decision must be applied consistently.

### Instructional verbs

Prefer direct verbs:

- jogue;
- adicione;
- escolha;
- compare;
- realize;
- gaste;
- reduza;
- recupere.

Avoid long nominal constructions such as:

- efetue a realização de;
- proceda com a aplicação de;
- faça a utilização de.

### Rule order

When possible, structure rules in this order:

1. trigger or condition;
2. acting subject;
3. action or roll;
4. modifier or target;
5. outcome;
6. duration or exception.

### Optionality

Translate permission and obligation carefully:

- **can / may** → pode;
- **must** → deve;
- **cannot** → não pode;
- **choose** → escolha;
- **may choose** → pode escolher.

### Character references

Prefer explicit game nouns over ambiguous pronouns when multiple creatures or effects are involved:

- a criatura;
- o alvo;
- o conjurador;
- o atacante;
- o personagem.

### Cross-references

Use stable section names and avoid changing terminology between headings and running text.

## Constraints and localization responses

| Constraint | Localization response |
|---|---|
| Repeated terms across hundreds of pages | Maintain a controlled bilingual glossary and reject unapproved variants. |
| Rules must be read during play | Use short sentences, visible triggers, and direct verbs. |
| Similar mechanics have different functions | Keep Teste de Atributo, Salvaguarda, and Jogada de Ataque distinct. |
| Character and spell levels can be confused | Use Círculo da Magia for spell classification. |
| English source uses imperial units | Convert to consistent, grid-friendly metric values. |
| Terms appear in narrow UI and sheet fields | Prefer compact labels such as CA, CD, PV, and Deslocamento. |
| Rules contain exceptions | Keep exception language explicit and close to the rule it modifies. |
| Dice expressions must remain functional | Preserve standard notation and mathematical operators. |
| Source text mixes explanation and instruction | Match the register to the function of each passage. |
| Players may search terms digitally | Use one approved Portuguese equivalent per English concept. |
