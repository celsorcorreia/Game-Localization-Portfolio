# Khaoz Age RPG — Narrative Localization & Worldbuilding Terminology Case Study

**Language pair:** Brazilian Portuguese → English  
**Content type:** Dark fantasy RPG lore, cosmology, terminology, naming systems, and narrative documentation  
**Project:** Original intellectual property  
**My role:** Creator, worldbuilder, narrative designer, localization designer, translator, terminology developer, and documentation author  
**Status:** Portfolio case study — Version 1.0

---

## Executive summary

*Khaoz Age RPG* is an original dark fantasy tabletop role-playing setting built around Sarkan, a world shaped by divine creation, ancient catastrophes, planar wounds, unstable magic, and the corruptive influence of khaosite.

This case study documents the localization of core worldbuilding terminology and representative lore from Brazilian Portuguese into English. The objective was not to flatten the setting into generic fantasy language, but to create an English version that preserves:

- the mythic and ominous narrative voice;
- the hierarchy between goddesses, entities, and titans;
- the identity of coined terms such as **khaosite**;
- the distinction between generic chaos and the setting-specific **Khaoz** vocabulary;
- the internal consistency of locations, events, divine titles, cultures, and supernatural phenomena;
- readability for players and game masters;
- terminology that can scale across a wiki, campaign guide, bestiary, character options, and future publications.

The resulting framework combines creative localization with terminology governance and long-form narrative QA.

## Project context

Sarkan is a dark fantasy world whose name derives from an ancestral expression meaning **“land of dragons.”** The name refers to an ancient truth: dragons were among the first conscious beings shaped to watch over the newly formed world.

Sarkan is influenced by elemental and cosmic forces such as fire, water, air, earth, order, and chaos. These forces may leak into mortal reality through planar fractures, arcane experiments, ancient cataclysms, and wounds left by beings from other planes.

The most feared of these forces is Chaos: a negative energy capable of corrupting minds, destroying bodies, and altering matter itself. After the release of **Khoz, Titan of Chaos**, this influence became more intense and unpredictable. In some regions, chaotic energy emerges from the ground as unstable purple crystals known as **khaosite**.

Khaosite represents one of Sarkan’s central contradictions: it is an extraordinary source of arcane power and a constant threat to sanity, physical integrity, and the natural balance of the world.

## Localization focus

The case study concentrates on five related areas:

1. **Narrative voice**  
   Preserving a sophisticated dark fantasy tone without creating unnecessarily dense English prose.

2. **World-specific terminology**  
   Retaining coined names and branded spellings while translating descriptive concepts naturally.

3. **Divine taxonomy**  
   Maintaining the setting’s distinction between Goddesses of the Pantheon, Entities, Titans, and the broader category of Divinities.

4. **Titles and epithets**  
   Localizing divine titles with consistent rhythm, capitalization, symbolism, and hierarchy.

5. **Cross-document consistency**  
   Ensuring that the same terms remain stable across lore entries, timelines, character options, rules, maps, cultures, and campaign material.

## Terminology architecture

### 1. Proper nouns retained

Names with strong setting identity remain unchanged:

- Sarkan
- Khaoz Age
- Khoz
- Eos
- Astralea
- Granador’ah
- Subterra

These terms are explained through context, glossaries, and lore rather than replaced with descriptive English names.

### 2. Descriptive terms localized

Concepts whose meaning is important to immediate player comprehension are translated:

- Whispering Mists
- Seasonal Jewels
- Court of Shadows
- The Rupture
- Imminence Marker
- Goddess of Space
- Mother of Stars

### 3. Branded Khaoz spelling controlled

The English localization distinguishes:

- **chaos / chaotic** — generic English concepts;
- **Khaoz / Khaotic** — setting-specific names, phenomena, and derived terminology;
- **Khoz** — the Titan of Chaos;
- **khaosite** — the unstable crystalline substance associated with chaotic corruption.

This prevents the brand spelling from spreading into every ordinary use of the English word *chaos*.

### 4. Divine categories remain distinct

The setting uses a controlled divine taxonomy:

- **Goddesses** belong to the Pantheon.
- **Entities** exist outside that organised divine structure.
- **Titans** embody primordial or catastrophic forces.
- **Divinities** is the gender-neutral collective term.

This distinction affects lore, character sheets, religious systems, bestiary entries, and campaign hooks.

## Key localization decisions

### khaosite → khaosite

**Alternatives considered:**

- chaos crystal
- khaoz crystal
- chaosite
- khaozite

**Decision:**

**khaosite** is retained because it is a core piece of the setting’s identity. A descriptive term such as *chaos crystal* would explain the object but erase its branded and cultural importance.

The chosen spelling also separates the substance from generic chaos while maintaining a visible relationship with Khaoz Age.

**Usage:**

> Khaosite is an unstable source of arcane power and a persistent threat to sanity, flesh, and the natural balance of Sarkan.

---

### Anomalia Khaosítica → Khaotic Anomaly

**Alternatives considered:**

- Chaotic Anomaly
- Khaositic Anomaly
- Khaoz Anomaly
- Chaos Distortion

**Decision:**

**Khaotic Anomaly** preserves the brand spelling while remaining immediately readable as an English adjective-plus-noun construction.

**Chaotic Anomaly** would sound like a generic unstable event. **Khaositic Anomaly** creates a stronger connection to khaosite, but narrows the concept because not every anomaly is necessarily a direct crystalline manifestation.

---

### Brumas Sussurrantes → Whispering Mists

**Alternatives considered:**

- Whispering Fogs
- Murmuring Mists
- The Whispering Veil
- Mists of Whispers

**Decision:**

**Whispering Mists** preserves the source image, the personification of the phenomenon, and the elegant dark fantasy rhythm.

*Mists* sounds more atmospheric and mythic than *fogs*. *Murmuring Mists* is lyrical, but weakens the unsettling implication that the phenomenon may be speaking directly to travellers.

---

### A Ruptura → The Rupture

**Alternatives considered:**

- The Fracture
- The Sundering
- The Breach
- The Breaking

**Decision:**

**The Rupture** is direct, severe, and flexible enough to describe both a historical cataclysm and a wound in reality.

*The Sundering* has a strong fantasy register but introduces a more conventional genre term that could weaken Khaoz Age’s own historical vocabulary.

---

### Eos, o Vazio → Eos, the Void

**Alternatives considered:**

- Eos, Void Incarnate
- Eos, the Emptiness
- Eos, the Abyss
- Eos of the Void

**Decision:**

**Eos, the Void** is compact and mythic. It preserves the ambiguity of Eos as both a being and the primordial absence from which existence emerged.

*The Abyss* would suggest a place or hellish depth. *The Emptiness* feels psychological rather than cosmological.

---

### Astralea, deusa do Espaço → Astralea, Goddess of Space

**Alternatives considered:**

- Astralea, Goddess of the Cosmos
- Astralea, Lady of Space
- Astralea, the Space Goddess
- Astralea, Goddess of the Firmament

**Decision:**

**Goddess of Space** precisely names her primordial domain and keeps the heading consistent with other divine entries.

*Goddess of the Cosmos* is evocative but broader than the source. *Goddess of the Firmament* overlaps with other celestial concepts and divine domains.

---

### Mãe das Estrelas → Mother of Stars

**Alternatives considered:**

- Mother of the Stars
- Star Mother
- Mother of Starlight
- The Stellar Mother

**Decision:**

**Mother of Stars** has the strongest mythic cadence. Removing *the* makes the title feel like an archetypal epithet rather than a literal description.

## Narrative localization examples

### Example 1 — Introducing Sarkan

**PT-BR**

> Sarkan, cujo nome deriva de uma expressão ancestral traduzida como “terra de dragões”, é um mundo marcado por uma história própria de criação, ruína e corrupção.

**English**

> Sarkan, whose name comes from an ancient expression meaning “land of dragons”, is a world shaped by its own history of creation, ruin, and corruption.

**Localization notes**

- *Marcado por* becomes **shaped by**, which is more natural and active in English worldbuilding prose.
- The three-part rhythm of **creation, ruin, and corruption** is preserved.
- The meaning of Sarkan is explained without translating the proper name itself.

---

### Example 2 — The contradiction of khaosite

**PT-BR**

> A khaosite é uma das maiores contradições de Sarkan: uma fonte praticamente ilimitada de poder arcano e, ao mesmo tempo, uma ameaça constante à sanidade, ao corpo e ao equilíbrio natural.

**English**

> Khaosite is one of Sarkan’s greatest contradictions: an almost limitless source of arcane power and, at the same time, a constant threat to sanity, flesh, and the natural balance.

**Localization notes**

- **Flesh** replaces a literal *body* to strengthen the dark fantasy register.
- The sentence preserves the contrast between temptation and corruption.
- **Natural balance** remains broad enough to cover ecosystems, magic, and cosmology.

---

### Example 3 — Eos and the primordial myth

**PT-BR**

> Antes que houvesse céu, mar ou chão, não havia nem mesmo o som ou a luz. Havia apenas Eos — o Primogênito, o Vazio em carne e sombra, a ausência que carregava em si todas as possibilidades do que ainda não existia.

**English**

> Before there was sky, sea, or earth, there was neither sound nor light. There was only Eos—the Firstborn, the Void made flesh and shadow, the absence that carried within itself every possibility of what did not yet exist.

**Localization notes**

- The opening retains its ceremonial cadence.
- **The Firstborn** preserves the title without overexplaining it.
- **The Void made flesh and shadow** keeps the paradoxical mythic image.
- The em dash is tightened according to English editorial style.

---

### Example 4 — Astralea’s essence

**PT-BR**

> Astralea é o espaço tornado consciência, a vastidão que separa, sustenta e organiza todas as coisas.

**English**

> Astralea is space made conscious—the vastness that separates, sustains, and orders all things.

**Localization notes**

- **Space made conscious** preserves the poetic compression of the source.
- **Orders** carries both organisational and divine-authority meanings.
- The em dash creates a controlled pause and avoids a heavier relative clause.

---

### Example 5 — Astralea’s doctrine

**PT-BR**

> Tudo está onde deve estar — e se não estiver, será colocado.

**English**

> Everything is where it belongs—and what is not will be set in place.

**Localization notes**

- **Where it belongs** sounds more doctrinal and less mechanical than a literal *where it should be*.
- The second clause preserves Astralea’s cold authority.
- **Set in place** reinforces her domains of distance, order, containment, and cosmic balance.

---

### Example 6 — The Whispering Mists

**PT-BR**

> As Brumas Sussurrantes cercam todo o arquipélago de Granytza e são uma das maiores Anomalias Khaosíticas permanentes já registradas em Sarkan.

**English**

> The Whispering Mists surround the entire Granytza Archipelago and are among the greatest permanent Khaotic Anomalies ever recorded in Sarkan.

**Localization notes**

- The proper geographic construction becomes **Granytza Archipelago**.
- **Among the greatest** reads more naturally than a literal singular comparison.
- All defined setting terms remain capitalised consistently.

## Voice and style guide

### Narrative register

The English version uses a sophisticated dark fantasy voice that remains readable during play and reference use.

Preferred qualities:

- mythic but not archaic;
- ominous without excessive ornamentation;
- precise when describing cosmology and rules;
- sensory when describing corruption, places, and anomalies;
- restrained when portraying divine authority;
- direct enough for game masters to scan during preparation.

### Sentence rhythm

Lore introductions may use longer, ceremonial sentences. Rules, adventure hooks, and reference entries use shorter structures.

A paragraph should not maintain the same elevated cadence when the player needs practical information.

### Capitalisation

Capitalise:

- named historical events: **The Rupture**;
- defined supernatural phenomena: **Whispering Mists**, **Khaotic Anomaly**;
- divine categories when used as formal classifications: **Goddess**, **Entity**, **Titan**;
- divine domains and titles when part of a heading or formal epithet;
- setting-specific actions or mechanics when treated as defined terms.

Use lowercase for:

- generic chaos;
- khaosite as a common substance;
- ordinary gods or divine concepts outside a formal title;
- descriptive uses that are not defined game terms.

### Proper names

Preserve accents, apostrophes, and coined spellings when they are essential to identity:

- Granador’ah
- Kha’oz
- Astralea
- Equinara
- Wallectia
- Zaleinya

Do not simplify names merely to make them look more conventionally English.

## Constraints and localization responses

| Constraint | Localization response |
|---|---|
| Large interconnected setting | Maintain a controlled glossary and track titles, places, cultures, events, and divine categories. |
| Mythic Portuguese prose | Recreate cadence and symbolism rather than translating syntax mechanically. |
| Extensive coined vocabulary | Retain proper nouns and branded terms while documenting pronunciation and meaning separately. |
| Similar generic and branded concepts | Distinguish chaos / chaotic from Khaoz / Khaotic. |
| Multiple divine categories | Use Goddess, Entity, Titan, and Divinity consistently according to lore taxonomy. |
| Wiki and campaign-book use | Balance literary voice with scan-friendly headings, summaries, and reference tables. |
| Long-term worldbuilding | Record approved terms, alternatives, risks, and cross-references in version-controlled documents. |
| Cultural and linguistic variation | Allow naming conventions to change by culture without breaking global terminology rules. |

## Outcome

This localization pass establishes a scalable English framework for *Khaoz Age RPG*.

The approved structure supports:

- world and region articles;
- cosmology and Pantheon entries;
- Titan and Entity profiles;
- character options;
- historical timelines;
- campaign guides;
- adventure hooks;
- bestiary entries;
- game mechanics involving khaosite and anomalies;
- future publication in English.

The case study demonstrates how narrative localization, terminology management, and worldbuilding design intersect in a large fictional universe. The goal is not only to produce evocative English prose, but to maintain a coherent system that remains usable across hundreds of interconnected entries.
